# Structure-aware edge repair

Use for reported fragments, dirty hems, fringe, jaggies or edge flicker. First understand the object; a pixel statistic cannot determine whether a detail belongs to it.

## Establish the intended structure

Inspect the full raw frame, then the affected component at useful magnification. Identify material, folded surfaces, trim, true silhouette, cast/contact shadows, overlaps and negative spaces. For a garment, follow the cloth into its hem: lace scallops and fold shadows can be legitimate; a disconnected green/gray layer beneath them may be a keying remnant. Preserve coherent lighting, texture and the chosen style. Pixel-art steps, strands, fur, translucent effects and detached particles are not automatically defects.

Compare the same source index through raw source, matte/RGBA, atlas, encoded video and target player. Inspect RGB and alpha on light and dark backgrounds. Distinguish source artifacts, matting errors, resampling, encoding and playback before changing anything.

## Repair the identified component

1. Define the reviewed repair region, foreground/material evidence and protected regions. Track a moving region when necessary. Coordinate/color thresholds are clip-specific evidence, not universal garment rules.
2. Use the original source to classify unwanted background. Despilled RGB may already have recolored green shadows as gray cloth; it is then unreliable evidence of object identity. Background connectivity is useful but does not by itself prove that every green edge is unwanted.
3. Remove confirmed non-object pixels rather than merely reducing their opacity. Preserve legitimate holes and occlusion boundaries. Resolve the silhouette from its surrounding material/shape, not from island area alone.
4. Repair only the altered boundary. Use limited alpha antialiasing and extend appropriate adjacent foreground RGB into newly partial pixels. Do not replace intact cloth RGB with a constant/nearest color across a broad band; this erases lighting and folds. Do not blur the whole frame or repeatedly smooth an already processed output.
5. Review continuous motion and loop/transition boundaries. If the edge changes incoherently across time, distinguish moving lace from flicker. Motion-aligned temporal treatment is an option only after segmentation is correct; validate occlusion and reject warped mismatches to avoid ghosting. Optical flow cannot establish semantic ownership or repair missing motion.

When the user says fragments remain, reopen structural diagnosis. Increasing erosion, island size or blur strength is not evidence of a better repair.

## Verification and batch operation

- Retain source hashes, frame mapping, canvas/pivot/contact/timing metadata, operation version and the actual region/mask criteria.
- Assert protected interior RGB and pixels outside the repair band are unchanged; separately inspect modified boundary lighting. Zero interior changes does not prove a correct silhouette.
- Decode exported files; verify frame count, timing, alpha and target rendering. A video reaching its end proves playback, not absence of flicker.
- Provide matched A/B previews at the same geometry, background, speed and encoding quality. Keep original, candidate, locally verified and user-accepted states separate. Fix caching with content-derived media versions.
- Before reusing a diagnostic image, match its recorded input/page hashes to the current asset and source index. A screenshot named `current` may predate a contour recovery; regenerate stale evidence instead of repairing an obsolete defect again. An applied repair recipe must not be overwritten by a retry against the already changed pixels: create a new run with an explicit baseline and preserve the necessary sparse undo.
- A library request means inventory every clip and report per-clip status. Group by material/pose only after inspection, use bounded per-component masks, and flag exceptions such as props or colored effects. Do not transfer one fixed ROI/threshold to unrelated frames or label a successful sample as all-library acceptance.
- For joins, follow the actual state machine as well as pose labels. An entry/hold/exit reaction deliberately has a different intermediate pose; compare entry-tail to hold-head and hold-tail to exit-head. Regenerate reachable playback paths from the current code/library, record skipped interaction-only clips, and inspect them separately. A canonical idle comparison or silhouette overlap score is a review cue, not proof of a bad transition or full-speed acceptance.
- Process offline; avoid adding runtime filters to compensate for defective assets. Use atomic outputs, resumable per-clip records and a disk-space guard. Keep necessary reversible pixel deltas while comparing/retrying; avoid routine full-application backups. Restore a known pre-filter state on retry/version upgrade rather than accumulating smoothing.

## Observed case: crouched lace hem

A 1080-square, 24 fps, 124-frame character source had dark green floor shadow under pink-white lace. A bright-key distance mask retained shadow; nearest-foreground despill made some of it resemble gray fabric. Source-connected green cleanup improved the result, but global small-island removal and local contour smoothing still left user-observed hem fragments/flicker. A temporal candidate was explored but was not accepted as the solution.

The accepted local preview instead scoped the actual lower hem, identified nearby pale lace in the raw source, and removed connected green backdrop remnants adjacent to that lace. It reconstructed only the affected boundary, protecting cloth interiors and neighboring components. All 124 frames retained protected interior and outside-region pixels; the user then confirmed the visible defect appeared gone. This accepts that preview, not every asset or the entire animation library. No character-specific region, color threshold or media is a default for this skill.

## Bundled repair tool

`python scripts/repair_edges.py --frames rgba-frames --remove-masks reviewed-removal-masks --protect-masks protected-detail-masks --out repaired-run`

All directories contain identically named PNGs. Removal masks mark confirmed unwanted pixels in white; protection masks override removal, preserving accepted light/shadow/material detail. Protection masks are optional, but deciding which pixels belong to the object remains part of the visual review. The tool preserves canvas and untouched pixels, removes the masked pixels completely, limits smoothing to the adjacent band, and losslessly verifies each output. Use `--smooth 0` for intentionally hard/pixel-native contours. Retain the original timing metadata and feed repaired frames to the existing export pipeline. The report deliberately says visual/export review is still needed; it is not a semantic defect detector. Existing video or atlas input should first be extracted at the original frame mapping, then repacked without altering timing/pivots.

For motion-dependent hair/garment overlap and unstable fractional alpha, continue with [fine-hair occlusion and temporal repair](hair-occlusion-temporal-repair.md); it separates source topology, matte clipping and color instability, and records the limits of the current candidate.

## Clipped fine detail needs recovery, not stronger removal

A later enlarged hair check exposed a different failure: a source hair strand was continuous, but the keyed alpha broke its tip into a floating dot. Deleting all small components cannot reconstruct this strand. Compare the exact source frame before classifying it as debris; a lace tip, hair strand or particle may belong to the subject even when the damaged matte makes it detached.

For reviewed neutral/dark fine detail against a green backdrop, `scripts/recover_chroma_detail.py` can reconstruct local alpha and unmix foreground RGB from the original RGB frame. It requires matched source frames, RGBA frames and explicit review masks, plus either measured `--key-rgb R G B` or per-frame `--key-masks`. Optional protection masks preserve accepted lighting and interiors exactly. Example:

```
python scripts/recover_chroma_detail.py --frames rgba-frames --source-frames source-rgb-frames --review-masks reviewed-hair-masks --key-masks known-backdrop-masks --protect-masks protected-interiors --out hair-recovery-candidate
```

This is a bounded green-key color model, not a general matting/semantic model. It assumes neutral/dark detail within the review region; do not apply it to green clothing, broad garment shadows, white trim or an entire character without separate evidence. Sample the actual decoded backdrop: nominal pure green may decode closer to `(5,239,2)`, and using `(0,255,0)` can leave a faint rectangular veil. Limit compression noise with a reviewed opacity floor, preserving real thin strands. Inspect the region boundary, adjacent trim, varied backgrounds and the full motion; a recovered still remains a candidate until its moving contour and export are checked.

## Preserve thin connected structures during antialiasing

A full-library source/original/final comparison found that a 3×3 morphological opening shortened a thin high heel and left a detached-looking endpoint. The original silhouette retained the heel's full length. Global opening is therefore not a safe generic beauty pass: the same problem can affect straps, hair, lace bridges and narrow props.

Use local antialiasing that preserves attached foreground centerlines. Add a regression fixture with a two-pixel-wide attached structure, checking its full length and connectivity, and verify the actual source/current contour at high magnification. Filling a small keying gap is distinct from deleting a spur: only fill when the matching source supports real foreground there. Do not close real holes where the source still shows backdrop. A Gaussian/small-component score alone cannot certify semantic correctness.

If a previous pass damaged structure, rebase from a verified original plus the accepted material repair before trying the replacement; do not stack another smoothing filter onto the damaged output. Version completion markers, make page/journal writes atomic, and require previews/exports to match the replacement version so old successful encodes cannot masquerade as current quality approval.

## Clean chroma, material-aware alpha and coherent geometry

When a clean chroma plate is requested, confirmed background is one specified RGB color, without generated floor shadows, gradients, stains or floating remnants. Preserve the immutable source. Recompose a reviewed repaired RGBA master onto that color for the clean plate; do not globally replace green pixels in clothing or reflective objects. Fully transparent pixels composite to the exact key; antialiased foreground boundaries necessarily mix foreground and key. Exact constant RGB is a lossless-master guarantee, not a guarantee after lossy video compression: inspect the decoded deliverable too.

Assign alpha from coverage and material, not a universal opacity threshold. Opaque skin, opaque cloth and shoes remain opaque; partially covered boundary pixels and genuinely translucent material retain graded alpha. Confirmed empty background is alpha zero. Distinguish a dark fold or cast shadow on the subject from transparency; do not punch holes in shadows or turn lace openings into opaque fabric. Inspect straight versus premultiplied alpha in the actual compositing pipeline to avoid dark/bright halos. RGB under fractional alpha must represent plausible foreground color without key spill. Changing invisible RGB alone does not fix a wrong alpha mask.

Classify edges by component: smooth fabric curves, crisp rigid shoe edges, scalloped lace and fine hair need different contours. Clean does not mean blurred, uniformly rounded, fully opaque or binary alpha. Preserve sharp corners, intentional texture and legitimate negative spaces while removing unmotivated jaggies/debris. Check silhouettes against perspective, foreshortening, cloth attachment, limb anatomy, overlap order and contact with supporting surfaces. Compare adjacent frames: repairs must follow actual motion, not crawl, pulse in opacity, detach or change shape at occlusions. Do not reshape a subject merely to improve a numeric smoothness score.

Record per-clip chroma, alpha/material, contour/geometry and temporal review separately. Encoding tests cannot certify these visual properties. Any unresolved structural or temporal defect keeps the clip in review, even when a single-frame repair looks clean.

## Preserve plausible motion blur

Fast hands, feet, cloth, props and camera movement can produce legitimate blurred contours. Infer whether blur belongs to the motion from the source sequence: direction and extent should follow the visible trajectory and speed, evolve coherently across neighboring frames, and respect occlusion and perspective. Do not demand a crisp frozen silhouette in every frame, classify all soft pixels as debris, or add blur merely to conceal a discontinuity. Honor deliberately stylized blur or sharp motion when requested.

Distinguish exposure-related blur from key spill, disconnected matte fragments, compression ringing, stale-frame trails and interpolation ghosts. Compare the same time through source, RGBA and decoded output. A broad continuous finger sweep in the source that becomes scattered tips after keying needs source-guided alpha recovery, not stronger erosion or isolated-component deletion. Normal-speed playback and enlarged adjacent frames answer different questions; inspect both when available.

Preserve fractional coverage through the blurred boundary and plausible foreground RGB beneath it. Remove green contamination without turning the sweep into opaque skin, erasing its outer extent, changing protected interior lighting or imposing an abrupt mask boundary. Use a region that follows the moving component; a fixed hand ROI from one frame is not a general mask. Recheck entry/exit of the repair region, overlap with clothing/face and loop/transition boundaries for new flicker. Binary GIF alpha cannot fully retain soft motion coverage: keep an RGBA master and verify the actual delivery format rather than claiming identical softness.

## Reject unintended flicker and position jumps

Unless explicitly requested as a style or deliberate action, flashes, disappearing frames, abrupt position/scale jumps and crawling alpha are defects. Check inside each clip, its repeated boundary, and reachable inter-clip transitions. Endpoint overlap alone does not certify continuous velocity, support/contact or plausible motion.

Trace source → repaired frames → layout metadata → decoded delivery → actual player. Share the same pivot, support reference, scale registration and fractional coordinates across exports and runtime; independent rounded placement can create a seam even with identical images. Correct proven reference mismatches with stable clip-level registration, never per-frame silhouette recentering or rescaling that erases intended movement. Re-export when metadata changes, and include layout dependencies in cache fingerprints.

For a buffered player, retain the previous rendered image until the incoming decoder has presented a frame. Arm presentation observation before starting playback; a fulfilled play/load promise or elapsed fixed timeout is not proof of visible pixels. A timeout should preserve the prior image and report/retry the failure, not expose an empty buffer. Review target-player changes separately from source-motion repairs.

## Library acceptance and delivery gate

Maintain one current inventory of requested clips and reachable transition paths, with separate evidence for repaired contours/material alpha, alignment, full-speed motion, loop closure, inter-clip joins and target playback. Record source/output revisions and the actual scope inspected. A middle-frame board, a decoded-frame count, a sampled carousel route or successful unit tests must not mark unexamined motion or paths as accepted. Include user-triggered interruptions, entry/exit and held states when the application supports them; report sampled route coverage separately from exhaustive coverage.

Review support points and apparent body scale in a shared camera coordinate system. Check joint paths, weight transfer, contact, near/far limb identity, occlusion and clothing/prop attachment through each transition, not only matching endpoint silhouettes. Preserve intentional motion, perspective changes and requested effects. Diagnose unexplained flashes, jumps or scale pulses through source, matte, layout and player before changing them; do not hide a broken path with repeated recentering, ghosted crossfades or stronger erosion.

After a repair, revalidate the changed clip and its affected loops/adjacent transitions, rebuild dependent previews and target exports, and verify that the served/installed output is that revision. Keep candidate, repaired, encoding-verified, motion-reviewed and accepted states distinct. Regenerate status reports from current evidence; remove obsolete running-process claims and invalidate acceptance when its dependent pixels, metadata or playback implementation change. An unrepaired visible defect or an unperformed required review remains unfinished.

When the user explicitly says “commit and push after everything passes,” treat that condition as part of delivery. Complete the requested repairs and relevant checks first, inspect the staged diff for unrelated edits, temporary candidates or sensitive source data, then commit and push the authorized deliverables and verify the remote result. Do not commit an incomplete asset batch as finished or claim a code/skill commit delivered repaired runtime assets. Separate local validation, commit, push, packaging and release status; commit/push does not authorize a release. This is a conditional user workflow, not a requirement to use Git or ask for approval in every media task.

## Opaque material loss beside shoes, straps and narrow gaps

Use this diagnostic when a stocking, skin surface or opaque garment reveals the backdrop, while nearby silhouettes remain smooth. Trace the exact source frame, pre-cleanup matte, current RGB/alpha and decoded delivery. An old matte is evidence, not ground truth: restoring all old alpha can restore real hair gaps and green floor shadows. Alpha-only filling is insufficient when a prior filter also erased RGB.

Separate positive material surfaces (including stockings over opaque skin), fine straps, shoe-opening shadows, outer silhouettes and actual negative spaces. Do not infer transparency from dark shading or a green reflection. A luminance-gradient boundary can select a dark fold *inside* the material; locate the actual foreground/background transition within a reviewed band instead. Confirm that band across the sequence, and change the method when occlusion or the pose changes.

The earliest available matte may already have keyed out green-reflected opaque material. Requiring its alpha to be fully opaque can therefore prevent a necessary repair. Inspect the matched raw source and define an explicit opaque-interior mask independent of that damaged alpha. Keep real limb gaps, shoe openings and mixed silhouette pixels outside it. `scripts/restore_opaque_material.py --frames rgba --source-frames source-rgb --opaque-masks reviewed-opaque-interiors --protect-masks protected-gaps --out candidate` restores only reviewed pixels whose current alpha is below 255. Its default `--rgb-policy preserve-valid` keeps existing straight RGB when alpha is nonzero and recovers matched source RGB where alpha was zero; use `--rgb-policy source` only for separately confirmed RGB damage. It preserves already opaque RGB and every unselected pixel exactly. Every frame requires an explicit binary review mask, including an empty mask when unchanged. This binary mask authorizes an interior repair; it is not a replacement for graded contour alpha. Character-specific color tests may propose such masks but cannot authorize them. Check repaired shading joins and continuous motion before acceptance.

For a damaged local contour, a source-supported curve or fitted contour with subpixel coverage is an option. Preserve legitimate corners, buckles, straps and openings; check fit overshoot, thin-gap closure and lost motion blur. Restore opaque interior RGB from the matched source only where missing or demonstrably corrupted. Confine despill and foreground-color extension to the affected mixed boundary. Blend repair joins in premultiplied space, and verify that joins neither thin an opaque surface nor introduce a dark ridge. Do not recursively smooth an already repaired output on retries.

Case-specific coordinates, chroma thresholds, spline tolerance and supersampling factors are not library defaults. In one side-lying example, local stocking-boundary recovery and separate footwear contour treatment improved deleted ankle surfaces and residual shoe fragments. That case does not validate the same masks for another clip or prove every source frame is defect-free. A chroma-derived external contour also cannot preserve enclosed genuine holes without explicit handling.

Test both positive and negative ownership: opaque material cores stay opaque; known background gaps stay clear; untouched regions remain byte-identical. Check source-selected negative-space samples rather than guessing their coordinates from a composite. Preserve original dimensions, pivots, source indices and timing; decode all exported frames. Track frame inspection, temporal measurements, full-speed visual review, installed revision and user acceptance separately. Library-wide alpha differences are candidates for review, not a count of confirmed defects or repaired clips.

Human segmentation can help reject restoration outside a likely opaque interior, but its score is not calibrated material truth. Keep its coarse mask out of final alpha; it can merge narrow gaps, miss lace, or include a contact shadow. A conservative intersection of sampled masks can narrow candidate eligibility, but does not certify intervening frames. Color-qualified component growth can still recover unwanted gray strips beside hair: compare those candidates with actual source geometry before activation.

### Reusable reviewed-boundary tool

Keep each contour search within one material and one visible boundary. When lace, a strap, a shoe or an overlapping limb enters that search, the strongest edge may switch owners between adjacent frames. Inspect the largest measured contour changes against the source; a smoother curve can still follow the wrong object. Split or shorten the reviewed band, or track an explicit per-frame protection mask. Do not suppress this ownership error with temporal blur, and do not interpret a numeric jump threshold as proof that real motion is wrong.

Use `scripts/repair_material_boundary.py` after identifying a source-supported local contour. It does not infer material ownership or replace the full matte. Supply matching native-sized RGBA and source RGB PNG sequences, plus a JSON recipe with `frames` mapping **every** filename to a list of reviewed regions (`[]` explicitly preserves a frame). Each region has `reviewed: true`, `startX`, a dense native-pixel `curveY` array (one y coordinate per x column), `bandInside` and `bandOutside`. The covered material must lie above this single-valued boundary; use a different representation for folds, multiple crossings, hair or enclosed holes. Optional matching `--protect-masks` keep confirmed gaps and intact details byte-identical. Region coordinates must follow actual motion; matching pose labels alone are insufficient.

```sh
.venv/bin/python scripts/repair_material_boundary.py --frames rgba --source-frames source-rgb --regions reviewed-regions.json --protect-masks protected --out candidate
```

The tool estimates fractional coverage with subpixel samples, preserves intact opaque RGB, recovers missing source RGB, and blends joins in premultiplied space. Despill is off by default; a reviewed `despillPixels` band enables limited green-channel suppression and must be judged against material lighting. It refuses unreviewed contours, incompatible dimensions, canvas-crossing bands and reuse of a nonempty output directory. It hashes source/input/output and verifies PNG decoding; full-speed motion, loop/transition behavior and decoded target exports remain separate required reviews. Do not label a whole clip repaired when only its contact contour was treated.

A contact silhouette may have a dark floor-shadow strip between the opaque subject and the green background. Stopping at the first clearly green pixel can wrongly make that strip part of the subject. Within an already reviewed material band, compare the last positive material samples, the local intensity transition and background onset; confirm the selected edge is the material boundary, not an internal fold or the shadow's outer boundary. Add a negative test for the floor shadow as well as a positive test for recovered material. Do not apply a contact-shadow rule to black footwear or other genuine dark foreground.

An overlap can expose a rear shoe as a dark strip directly beneath a pale foreground calf. That strip belongs to the character even when it resembles a floor shadow. Verify limb ownership in the raw sequence before removing it. A skin-only lower curve can erase the rear shoe or jump onto its boundary; stop that candidate and split material/protection masks. A local foreground/background compositing fit may propose recovery of mixed pixels when both source anchors are visible, but a green-channel threshold alone does not approve opacity or distinguish shoe from shadow. Preserve already opaque RGB, test a real negative-space gap, and review all changed frames before activation.

### Separate material holes from contact-boundary residue

For holes that remain dark after opacity repair, inspect RGB independently. A nearest-core keyer may have copied black cloth into pale skin or white fabric before reducing alpha. Restoring alpha alone then reveals that contaminated color. Compare the exact source index, current straight RGB, alpha and composite. Recover source RGB only inside separately reviewed damaged material pixels; preserve valid despilled RGB, contact shadows and texture elsewhere. An enclosed-hole mask does not cover an adjacent opaque RGB scar or damaged silhouette: record and repair those as separate scopes instead of declaring the whole region clean.

Bind approvals and playback evidence to the actual atlas content, not only clip names, layout metadata or asset-version labels. Use a monotonic source-duration clock in diagnostic players; asynchronous decode plus repeated relative delays can introduce apparent slow motion or judder. Record missed presentations separately from source-frame defects. A lossless decode check, a running video element, sampled frames and full-speed perceptual acceptance prove different things.

A single complaint can contain two opposite matte errors: an opaque stocking has lost coverage, while the lower hand/cuff silhouette retains background shadow and has missing foreground. Diagnose each component in the matched source sequence. Increasing alpha across the whole region restores the stocking but can strengthen the unwanted floor shadow; reducing alpha across it removes the shadow but erases the hand. Use separate reviewed interior and boundary repairs with independent protection masks.

For a hand resting on dark hair or cloth, follow the visible occlusion order. Dark foreground beneath fingers is not automatically an empty finger gap. Conversely, exposed green ground beside the cuff is not cloth. A fixed column-based contour is only suitable while the reviewed surface remains above a single-valued boundary; split the region when fingers, lace or another limb cause multiple crossings. Missing foreground/background anchors must leave that column unmodified and flagged for review, never silently count as successful repair.

A local foreground/background color fit can produce purple fringes or thin vertical spikes when its foreground sample changes material between columns. Reject those artifacts during the all-frame crop review. For a confirmed opaque contact contour, an explicitly source-supported subpixel boundary with locally extended foreground color can be preferable; preserve genuine motion blur rather than forcing this sharp-edge treatment onto a moving hand. Check corners, adjacent untouched columns and the repair region's fade joins. Do not copy any example's coordinates, color thresholds, fitting window or coverage width into unrelated clips.

Verify a known damaged material pixel becomes appropriately opaque without losing its source shading, a known true gap remains transparent, and pixels outside the reviewed patch remain byte-identical. Recheck every changed frame on dark and light backgrounds, then normal-speed playback and affected joins. Record these checks separately: a repaired still and successful atlas/GIF decode do not establish full-speed acceptance. Preserve failed candidates as rejected evidence when useful; never promote them merely because a later local version passed.

For an entire library, enumerate every clip and component requiring review before applying repairs. Track inspected-normal regions separately from repaired regions; intact material should not be changed to make a progress count increase. Refresh source/output hashes after each accepted patch, invalidate dependent old previews and acceptance, and verify the installed/served version after rebuilding. One clip's improved hand or ankle is evidence for the diagnostic method, not approval of unattended global rekeying.

### Material junctions can escape enclosed-hole detection

An enclosed-hole scan is only one diagnostic. A valid light frill over dark cloth may contain partly transparent pixels near their junction; green spill or shading can make an entire connected component fail a conservative color test. A zero-candidate result therefore does not establish intact material. Compare source RGB, output alpha and an explicit high-contrast composite at the reported location. Inspect the opaque surface underneath trim as well as the trim itself. Distinguish a dark seam in RGB from missing alpha before editing.

Use a separately reviewed moving region for these junctions, protect the true outer contour and hair/neck gaps, and recover only demonstrably opaque material from its source. Do not expand global color thresholds to catch one junction. Review every changed frame and preserve source shading; alpha restoration must not paint the seam white. Check the installed assets against the inspected file hashes and distinguish source-frame evidence from confirmation of the user's exact displayed frame.

### Restore coverage without undoing valid color work

Judge opacity through the complete visible layer stack. Openwork trim over opaque cloth or skin cannot reveal the external backdrop: the underlying surface contributes opaque coverage. The same trim suspended over empty space may have real holes. Shadows, pale green reflections and dark seams are color evidence, not automatic transparency. Inspect the raw sequence and occlusion order before selecting a repair mask; a filled silhouette or segmentation model alone cannot establish this distinction.

Separate alpha damage from RGB damage. When existing RGB already contains valid despill and intact lighting, restore only the reviewed opaque coverage and retain that RGB. Replacing every partially transparent pixel with raw green-screen RGB can reintroduce spill. Recover matched source color only where RGB was erased or demonstrably corrupted, then inspect its color joins. Confirm that the pipeline uses straight RGBA before retaining RGB unchanged; premultiplied data requires the corresponding conversion. Preserve graded outer coverage and actual gaps. Do not use an opaque-interior repair as a whole-frame alpha threshold.

For fragmented damage, review full-frame boards alongside native-size crops on dark and light backgrounds; rendering a separate large image for every tiny component can waste memory without clarifying structure. Record exactly which frames and components were inspected. Successful static review, lossless repacking and a unit test do not substitute for continuous playback and transition review.

Include a regression fixture with a green-reflected opaque cloth region and a genuine green gap. A keyer that discards every green-excess pixel far from a neutral-color core can erase that cloth even when its geometric coverage is complete. The fixture should require restoration only inside the reviewed material mask, retain valid despilled RGB, protect the gap, and leave outer graded coverage byte-identical. Keep mask/protection-mask and algorithm hashes with the input/output hashes so retries cannot silently reuse an obsolete material decision.

Nonzero alpha does not prove that the stored RGB is valid. A nearly erased cloth pixel can contain black RGB; raising only its alpha makes a new black dot. Compare source/current/proposed RGB and alpha before approving the patch. Where source-supported opaque cloth has separately confirmed RGB damage, use a narrowly reviewed RGB-recovery mask with `--rgb-policy source` while alpha is still damaged, then apply alpha-only recovery to the remaining reviewed interior. Do not apply source-color replacement to the entire clip. Record this exception and verify that the recovered local color blends with adjacent shading on both dark and light backgrounds.

If a previous alpha-only pass already made the damaged color fully opaque, use the separate `--rgb-damage-masks` input to `restore_opaque_material.py`. These explicitly reviewed binary masks must be contained in the reviewed opaque interior; protection masks take precedence. This opt-in can recover RGB on already opaque pixels without changing their alpha, and reports/hashes the exception separately. Default processing still preserves already opaque RGB. Review the damaged region against its source first; darkness alone never establishes corruption in black garments, footwear, seams or shadow.

Nearest-opaque-pixel color extension must respect material ownership. Near a white frill over a black sleeve, a geometrically close opaque sample can belong to the sleeve and paint a dark notch into the frill. Inspect large source/output color disagreements even after alpha is fully opaque. Recover the confirmed damaged foreground from the aligned source, with any necessary despill confined to that reviewed material; do not resample across the seam or blur both materials together. Color-difference thresholds only locate candidates and must not erase real dark embroidery, folds or cast shadows.
